## Argument

The aggressive analyst's "use the full $500 day one, don't hold back" pitch ignores that the user's own rules already encode the conservative view and were set before this debate — the debate doesn't get to relitigate the cap, only the conviction behind it, and I see no basis here to lean toward the aggressive end of that cap. "6x oversubscribed" is one narrative echoed through wire-to-wire reporting and retail reposting the same figure — the news analyst never traced it to the S-1/A or an underwriter statement. Heavy oversubscription at pricing is a textbook setup for a day-one pop followed by a fade once lock-up and valuation reality bite, which is exactly when a stop-limit is most exposed to a gap-through that burns most of the deployed capital, not the ~$75 the aggressive case treats as the base case.

The aggressive dismissal of macro as "already priced in" is unfalsifiable — a withdrawn higher draft tells us banks found a pricing error, not that macro risk is resolved. A hot inflation print, a 10Y yield spike, and 1% cut odds are confirmed headwinds for a richly valued, unprofitable-growth name regardless of book quality, and they argue for restraint, not full allocation. The unresolved lock-up conflict (up to 10% of non-founder shares sellable day one) is a live overhang the aggressive case doesn't address at all. I'd go further than the trader's plan: treat the 0.90x alert and the 15% stop as the real risk controls, keep both day-one orders exactly as sized — no upsizing — and resist any temptation to front-load conviction into day one that the rules already say should wait for a named, confirmed positive trigger in week two.
