## Argument

Both sides are arguing to move inside a cage that was deliberately built to need no adjustment. The aggressive analyst says use the full $500 day one because the book is covered; the conservative says lean toward not using it because macro is hostile and lock-ups are murky. Both are right about their facts and wrong to push a lever that the trader's plan — and the user's fixed rules — already set at a specific, deliberate value: two $250 DAY limits at 1.05x and 1.15x P, no more, no less, contingent on the 1.30x gate. The aggressive case treats a covered book as resolving macro risk; it doesn't, it just means current pricing cleared at this range, which the bear-side macro facts (confirmed inflation print, yield spike, 1% cut odds) still sit on top of. The conservative case treats macro as a reason to shrink below the plan; but the plan's $500 cap, 15% stop, and 0.90x alert already price in exactly this kind of hostile-macro, pop-then-fade scenario — shrinking further isn't prudence, it's double-counting a risk the rules already discount.

The actual edge case worth deciding is the aggressive analyst's "don't wait for week two, day one has the only clean stop-defined entry." That's a fair point about optionality, but it argues for following the plan as written, not exceeding it — day one already is the full allocation the rules permit. Where I'd push back on everyone: the real unresolved variable isn't sizing, it's the lock-up-conflict and pricing-date gaps flagged by the news analyst. Neither extreme position addresses re-verifying those before the gate check. Recommendation: execute the trader's plan exactly as sized, treat the stop and alert as load-bearing per the conservative's flag, and resist the aggressive push to front-load conviction beyond what's already allocated.
