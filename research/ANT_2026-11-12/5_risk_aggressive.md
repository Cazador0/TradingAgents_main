## Argument
**"Exit liquidity" is answered by the plan itself.** It buys only after Nov 18 pricing, only on limits, only at or below 1.3P. If allocated holders flip into a weak bid, the tranches do not fill and the cost is zero. Worst case is $1,000 (Plan A) or $300 (Plan B) of $2,000, with $1,000 in reserve.

**Cash is not free.** This is a record deal with a reportedly covered book, ~20% retail allocation, and shut-out buyers who must use the open market. The bull report calls that bid plausible, though unquantified. H1 revenue was ~$16.3B against ~$1.16B a year earlier. Underweight is itself a bet that this will not be re-rated.

**The supplier placement cuts both ways.** Yes, $26B of Class N is circular-style demand. It is also $26B of committed capital at the IPO price from seven parties who need the company to succeed.

**Macro is already being marked down.** Polymarket cut upper first-day brackets about 11pp, so the bar for the debut is lower. Hike odds of ~77-80% and a 10y at 4.84% are real, but the deal is reportedly clearing anyway.

**Where I would go further than the trader.**
- Keep the $300 above-1.3P starter. The manager calls it a chase, but it is labeled, dollar-capped, and a $300 loss is survivable. Dropping it means cash in the one trade that could matter.
- Act fast on the re-rate triggers (company-confirmed oversubscription, a stable first week). Waiting for perfect confirmation gives up the upside this plan exists to capture.

**Stops are the real control.** The 15% stop-limit caps Plan A loss near $150, and the gap-down risk is already disclosed. Unknown lock-ups and CPI are reasons to size small, not to stand aside.
