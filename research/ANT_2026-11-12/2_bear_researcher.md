## Reasoning
- Inputs: sentiment and news reports only. No Bull argument file existed, so I rebut the bullish case as the reports themselves frame it (strong book, retail enthusiasm, scarcity).
- Frame: the user has no allocation. They buy on the open market on Nov 19 at whatever the opening print is, not at the $70-$78 range. Every bullish datapoint about demand is mostly a case for the allocation, not for the price an open-market buyer pays.
- Weighting: the demand evidence is secondhand or thin (anonymous CNBC sourcing, 13 labeled StockTwits posts, ~$1M Polymarket volume). The risk evidence (macro, size, financing structure, losses) is sourced to filings or the Fed.
- Gaps I will not fill: comparable first-day performance, oversubscription multiple, lock-up terms, the Oct CPI print, and the full Polymarket distribution.

## Argument
**1. The bull's demand story does not transfer to an open-market buyer.** The "book is covered" claim is anonymous, single-relayed, and unconfirmed by the company, with no oversubscription multiple. Retail chatter is about getting shares ("request 500... prob get 10"), not about post-listing value. That is allocation FOMO, and the sentiment report itself warns it fades if allocations are generous. If demand is strong, allocated holders flip into the open and the user is the exit liquidity. If demand is weak, the user holds a falling stock.

**2. The demand is partly manufactured.** Seven compute suppliers are buying $26B of non-voting Class N stock at the IPO price, with no usual lock-up. The news report calls this supplier-linked, circular-financing-style demand, not organic public demand. The retail sentiment report notes the same circular-financing concern.

**3. Record size, no comparables.** About $64.4B raised, ~825M shares plus a 123.75M-share greenshoe, at $1.26T-$1.41T implied. The news analyst says this is the largest IPO ever by a wide margin and that smaller-float "pop" arguments do not apply. Nobody can say a day-one pop is likely, and the comparable data was never retrieved.

**4. Traders are already marking expectations down.** Polymarket's upper IPO-day market-cap brackets fell about 11pp while lower ones rose, after the range reset. The "<$1.25T" bracket sits at 18%. The market is thin, but the direction is not bullish.

**5. Fundamentals leave no cushion.** H1 revenue of ~$16.3B is large growth, but the company itself warned growth may slow. Net loss is ~$80.7B. The reportedly non-cash fair-value split is unverified. Spending commitments of ~$518B come from a low-quality source and are unverified. Valuation at ~$1.3T is built on extrapolating hypergrowth.

**6. Macro is a headwind for long-duration paper.** Fed at 3.75-4.00% with a ~77-80% priced chance of a December hike, 10-year at 4.84%, and the S&P's worst week since April 2025. Retail posts on both sides cite liquidity drain, and "all the liquidity... is on the sidelines" is a stated worry.

**7. Execution flags.** A withdrawn $70-$80 term sheet had a pricing error, and the valuation ($1.3T vs. $1.4T) and lead-bank details conflict across sources. The Nov 18/19 timing is press-sourced, not company-confirmed.

**Bottom line for a $2,000 day-one buy:** the asymmetry is poor. The user pays the post-open price, after insiders and allocated holders have taken the first-move gains, and carries the risks above. If the user still buys, the reports support only a limit order (never a market order into the open), a small position, and an ongoing willingness to wait for a settled price. I do not see support in these reports for chasing the debut.
