## Argument
The trader's HOLD with a conditional entry is directionally right. Four parts of it still carry more risk than the evidence supports.

**1. Drop Plan B.** The manager called the $300 above-1.5x starter "the chase the bear warns about" and advised dropping it. The trader kept it, now triggered at open above 1.3P. If the open gaps above 1.3P, demand is strongest and allocated holders have the most reason to sell into the bid. Buying after a 10:30 "settle" is a bet that the spike holds, and no report gives a basis for that. The user's preference for a position over cash is a preference, not evidence. Staying in cash costs nothing but a missed move. A $300 loss is small, but it is the one order that breaks the user's own ceiling.

**2. The bull's "bounded loss" is not a stop.** The loss is bounded only by the amount deployed. The trader's own text says a gap-down can fill well below the trigger or not at all. A stop-limit 2% under the stop can simply not fill. The honest worst case is $1,000, not $150.

**3. Plan A's ladder is a falling-knife ladder.** T1 to T4 span 1.05P to 1.30P. If the open is weak, the lower rungs fill first and the stock keeps falling. The ladder buys into weakness unless the 0.9P alert actually halts it, and DAY limit orders cannot be paused once resting. I would cap day-one exposure at $500 (two tranches), not $1,000, since the manager's own wording was ambiguous.

**4. The 1.3P ceiling is arbitrary.** It rests on no comparable, and the news report says none exist at $64B. P also includes a $26B supplier placement that the news report calls circular-financing-style demand, so "at or above range" overstates organic demand.

**5. Macro has not cleared.** December hike odds of about 77-80%, a 10-year at 4.84%, and a tech selloff all hit long-duration paper. Oct CPI is unknown.

**Adjustments:** remove Plan B, cap day one at $500, require confirmed pricing at or above $70 before any order, and hold at least $1,500 through the first week.

Not investment advice.
