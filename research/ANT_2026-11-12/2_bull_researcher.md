- Role prompt read (bull_researcher.py). Inputs: sentiment and news reports only. 2_bear_researcher.md did not exist, so I pre-empt the bear points the reports raise.
- Decision framed: buying on the open market on day one via Fidelity, $2,000, no allocation. The case must work at the day-one market price, not at $70-$78. The reports give no day-one price forecast or comparables, so I claim none.
- Strongest evidence: H1 revenue ~$16.3B vs ~$1.16B a year earlier; book reportedly covered at the range. Concessions: demand claim is single-source, sentiment sample is thin, loss split unverified.

## Argument
**The business is real and growing fast.** H1 2026 revenue was about $16.3B against $1.16B a year earlier. A $1.3T-$1.4T valuation is a bet on that growth continuing, though the company itself warned it may slow. The S-1 is public, so you are not buying blind.

**Demand looks strong where it matters for a day-one buyer.** CNBC (anonymous sources, medium-low confidence) says the book is covered at the range. Retail allocation is targeted at about 20%, and retail posters expect tiny fills ("request 500... prob get 10"). Anyone shut out of the IPO has to buy in the open market. That is a plausible source of day-one bid, though the reports do not quantify it.

**Sentiment leans modestly positive.** StockTwits labeled messages split 11 bullish to 2 bearish. The report scores it 6.0/10, "Mildly Bullish", confidence low, with n=13 labeled and most posts about getting shares, not the post-listing price. I will not oversell it.

**The loss headline is probably accounting.** The ~$80.7B H1 net loss is reported as largely from higher fair value of convertible bonds. One low-quality source puts the non-cash share near 89%. That is unverified, so treat it as probable, not established.

**Pre-empting the bear.**
- *Circular financing ($26B Class N placement from seven suppliers, no usual lock-up).* Fair: it is not organic demand. But it is disclosed, non-voting and priced at the IPO price. Be aware it is not subject to the usual lock-up.
- *Size (825M shares, ~$64B raise).* Real, and there are no true comparables. But Polymarket traders already cut upper-bracket first-day cap odds after the range reset, so some caution is priced in. That market is thin (~$1M volume).
- *Macro (December hike odds ~77-80%, 10y at 4.84%, tech selloff).* A genuine headwind. The deal is reportedly clearing in spite of it.
- *Liquidity drain from NVDA/MSFT/QQQ.* Retail posts cite it, but it is anecdotal.

**What this means for your $2,000.** The bull case is not "it pops". It is a high-growth, record-size deal with a reportedly covered book and a structural day-one retail bid. Sensible implications:
- A $2,000 loss is bounded.
- Use a limit order, not a market order, on a day-one open that may be volatile.
- Consider scaling in rather than buying all at once.
- Treat Nov 18 pricing as the checkpoint. Pricing at or above the range supports the thesis. A downward revision weakens it.

**Gaps I cannot fill:** oversubscription multiple, lock-up terms for other holders, Oct CPI, first-day comparables.
